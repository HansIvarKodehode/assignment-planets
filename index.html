Mass: 1.988*10^30 kg

Orbital radius: 24 000 - 28 000 light-years

Galactic period: 225-250 million years

Venus

Mean radius: 6 051.8 km

Mass: 4.867*10^24 kg

Natural Satelites: None

Orbital radius: 0.72332 AU

Orbital period: 224.7 days

Rotational Period: 243.0226 days, retrograde

If hell is real, then Venus is certainly a contender for where it is. Let's start with the atmosphere. Its ~96.5% CO2 and ~3.5% Nitrogen, with traces of sulfuric compounds, and at surface level the preassure is about 93 times higher than on Earth. This dense atmoshpere causes the strongest greenhouse effect in the known universe. This greenhouse effect traps the outcomming heat from the planets volcanic activity, resulting in temperatures around 462 °C. The first 40-75 km of the atmosphere is almost pure CO2, and above that are dense clouds suspected to be made of sulphuric acid. The atmoshpere also moves faster than the planet rotates, resulting in constant winds. In the higher layers of the atmosphere this reults in 300kph winds as standard, but in the lower layers the amtosphere is so dense that the winds are only a few kph, but it has considerate power. It becomes the difference between a getting hit by a very fast pinpong ball and a very slow truck.
So: heavy volcanic activity, sulphuric rain, monstrous winds, and a preassure cooker atmosphere. Let's not go to Venus.
And if that wasn't enough: It rotates around the sun the opposite direction of almost everyone else.

Terra, Tellus, Earth

Mean radius: 6 371 km

Mass: 5.972*10^24 kg

Natural Satelites: 1, Luna

Orbital radius: 1AU, or 149 598 023 km

Orbital period: 365.256 days

Rotational Period: 23.934 hours, currently

Our own little slice of paradise. Seemingly the only rock in this solar system that has an actual atmosphere, water, and is in the Suns habitable zone. Temperatures vary between +60 and -100(counting windchill). The official name is not truly set, as both Tellus and Terra are viable choices, in true Roman fassion. In Roman mythology Tellus is often used for the spirit, god, or personification of the planet, while Terra is often used for the actual planet, but are also used interchangably.

Mars

Mean radius: 3 389.5 km

Mass: 6.4171*10^23 kg

Natural Satelites: 2, Phobos and Deimos

Orbital radius: 1.38 AU

Orbital period: 686.980 days

Rotational Period: 1.027 days

The red planet. Close to Earth in both size and mass, and even has water. It's frozen, but it's there. The atmosphere is however very thin, it wasn't always the case as it once been dense enough to sustain liquid water, but atmospheric leakage is no joking matter.
Between what it was, with liquid water, and what it could one day be: it is a hotbed for research. Currently the surface, and subsurface is being scanned with radar instruments to learn more about it.

Jupiter

Mean radius: 69 886 km

Mass: 1.898*10^27 kg

Natural Satelites: 115

Orbital radius: 5.2038 AU

Orbital period: 4 332.59 days

Rotational Period: 9.925 hours

Jupiter, second only to the Sun itself in size and influence. A gas giant, composed mostly of hydrogen and helium, with traces of methane and ammonia, it has a multipart core, an inner core of diffuse heavier elements, and an outer core of liquid metallic hydrogen. The contractions of Jupiters inner workings currently produce more heat than it recieves from the Sun, it is however not a brown dwarf.
It's theorized that Jupiter once migrated through the solar system, traveling from its origiinal orbit, inn close towards the Sun, and out again to its current orbit, having cast fledgling planets out of the system, and captured others. This would make Jupiter heavily responsible for the current design of the solar system, and explain why it has as many and massive moons as it has.
Among those moons are Ganymede, Europa, Callisto and Io, all of whom are almost as large as Mercury, except Ganymede which is larger, but has less mass. Io has immense volcanic activity. Callisto is riddled with craters, to the point it is theorized to be one of the oldest planetoids in the system. Ganymede is the only natural satelite in the system with its own magnetic field, and Europa has liquid water under an solid layer of ice, which also makes it the roundest object in the solar system.
The large red spot on jupiters surface is an enormous hurricane

Saturn

Mean radius: 58 232 km

Mass: 5.683*10^26 km

Natural Satelites: 293, with an unknown number of moonlets

Orbital radius: 9.586 AU

Orbital period: 10 755.7 days

Rotational Period: 10h:33m:38s

Saturn, best known for its rings, made of ice and assorted rock. Now, all the giants have ring systems, but none are as grand as Saturn's. By itself it is not as interesting as Jupiter, however it is not alone.
Among its countless moons we have Titan, the second largest natural satelite in the system, and it is an odd one. Titans surface comes in layers. On the very top is a layer of assorted ice, including liquid hydrogen ice. Beneath that is a layer of liquid water that is rich in ammonia. While that is interesting the next layer is more interesting: A planet spanning layer of liquid hydrocarbons. A moonwide ocean of oil. And then planet. But oceans of liquid hydrocarbons, with a layer of liquid ammonia rich water on top. Imagine how life could have evolved in these conditions. Assuming there is any.

Uranus

Mean radius: 25 362 km

Mass: 8.68099*10^25 kg

Natural Satelites: 29

Orbital radius: 19.19 AU

Orbital period: 30 688 days, also retrograde

Rotational Period: 0.718 days

We have yet to change its name to Urectum. It also travels in a retrograde orbit, and is has the lowest temperature found naturally in the solar system, sitting at -244 °C(49K). It has a rocky core, with a hydrogen/helium atmosphere. Due to the rocky core it is considered an ice giant, rather than a gas giant. It is known to have seasonal weather patterns.

Pluto, the former planet. There are several classification cirterias Pluto has failed. Its mass is not sufficent to round out, and it also has less surface area than Russia.
It really is a dwarf planet.
Also, fun fact: It was discovered in 1930, and reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006. Pluto didn't get to be a planet for a full third of a Pluto year.
